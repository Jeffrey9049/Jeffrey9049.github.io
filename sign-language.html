Taiwan Sign Language Research

I work on computational methods for Taiwan Sign Language (TSL), combining motion-based representations with linguistic annotations for recognition and corpus analysis.

Research Overview

Sign-language understanding requires information about handshape, palm orientation, location, movement, and temporal context. My research considers how these visual and linguistic dimensions can be represented and used in data-driven models, particularly when labeled datasets are limited.

At the Graduate Institute of Linguistics, National Chung Cheng University, I contribute to multimodal corpus processing and machine-learning-based annotation and recognition workflows.

Current Work

Landmark-Based Sign Recognition

We extract body and hand landmarks from recorded signing and use temporal sequence models to recognize signed lexical items. Existing offline experiments use a bidirectional GRU and compare landmark-only representations with approaches incorporating aligned ELAN linguistic information.

MediaPipe · Spatiotemporal landmarks · Bidirectional GRU · PyTorch

Linguistically Informed Modeling

ELAN annotation tiers provide complementary descriptions of a sign, including gloss, handshape, palm orientation, location, and movement. I am interested in whether structured feature processing, attention mechanisms, and multi-task supervision improve recognition and generalization.

ELAN · Multimodal learning · Feature attention · Multi-task learning

Corpus Analysis

In addition to lexical recognition, I study computational workflows for analyzing annotated Taiwan Sign Language corpora, including semantic roles, discourse reference, and relationships between linguistic labels and movement observations.

Corpus processing · Linguistic annotation · Semantic analysis

Methods and Evaluation

Data and Modeling

  • Temporal landmark sequences from video
  • Gloss-aligned ELAN annotation tiers
  • Sequence recognition and representation learning
  • Feature-specific and multi-task approaches

Evaluation Questions

  • Top-1 and Top-5 lexical accuracy
  • Generalization across signers
  • Visually similar sign vocabulary
  • Robustness across recording conditions
