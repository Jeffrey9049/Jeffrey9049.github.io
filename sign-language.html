Taiwan Sign Language Research

Understanding, translation, and generation through computer vision, multimodal learning, and language modeling.

Research Overview

My long-term research focuses on two complementary problems: translating Taiwan Sign Language (TSL) into written Chinese and generating linguistically meaningful signing through digital avatars. These problems require an understanding of both the visual dynamics of signing and the linguistic structure that gives those movements meaning.

At the Graduate Institute of Linguistics, National Chung Cheng University, I work on annotated TSL corpora, multimodal representations, and machine learning. My current research investigates robust motion capture, occlusion-aware pose reconstruction, linguistically meaningful spatial representations, discourse-level language modeling, and skeletal motion generation. I view isolated sign recognition as a foundation for broader language understanding rather than as the final translation task.

Research Directions

Robust Motion Capture and Occlusion-Aware Hand Pose Estimation

Accurate motion capture is critical for both sign language recognition and generation. Small differences in handshape, finger articulation, palm orientation, location, and movement can change the linguistic interpretation of a sign. Conventional landmark estimation can become unreliable when hands overlap, fingers are self-occluded, or a hand passes behind the body.

I investigate how temporal context, anatomical constraints, and complementary pose observations can improve the reliability of landmark sequences and support inference of partially observed hand configurations. Rather than treating missing landmarks as arbitrary interpolation targets, the goal is to reconstruct plausible, temporally consistent motions while accounting for uncertainty when the visual evidence is insufficient.

Computer vision · Hand pose estimation · Temporal modeling · Occlusion-aware reconstruction

Status: Ongoing research and prototype exploration.

Linguistically Informed Sign Language Recognition

My recognition research investigates how spatiotemporal landmarks and linguistic annotations can be combined to distinguish signed lexical items. Existing offline experiments use bidirectional GRU models to compare landmark-only representations with representations incorporating aligned ELAN annotations, including handshape, palm orientation, location, and movement.

Building on these experiments, I am exploring feature-specific representations, attention mechanisms, and multi-task learning to investigate whether linguistic structure improves recognition of visually similar signs and generalization across signers. The current isolated-sign recognition workflow serves as an experimental foundation for future continuous-sign understanding.

Spatiotemporal representation learning · BiGRU · Transformers (exploratory) · ELAN · Multimodal learning

Status: Existing baseline experiments; further modeling and evaluation are ongoing.

Spatial Semantics and Reference Modeling in Sign Language

Signing space is not merely a geometric environment for describing motion; it can also encode linguistic and discourse information. Signers may establish spatial loci for referents, reuse these locations in subsequent utterances, and express relationships through pointing, directional movement, and the spatial organization of signs. Consequently, learning the coordinates of individual joints alone is insufficient for understanding the meaning of signed discourse.

I aim to investigate how models can learn the linguistic significance of spatial configurations from motion sequences and aligned annotations. A central challenge is to distinguish meaningful spatial relationships from variation caused by signer anatomy, body position, viewpoint, and recording conditions. Potential approaches include body-relative spatial representations, relational modeling of hands, body, and referential loci, spatiotemporal attention, and discourse-level memory for tracking established references. These approaches will be explored in relation to annotated Taiwan Sign Language data, rather than assuming that physical proximity or movement direction alone determines linguistic meaning.

This research connects both of my long-term objectives: translation requires the interpretation of spatial reference and relationships across utterances, while sign language generation requires the establishment, maintenance, and consistent reuse of meaningful spatial locations in avatar motion. It also complements my participation as a research assistant in the funded project Information Structure and Reference Tracking in Taiwan Sign Language.

Spatial semantics · Referential loci · Spatial reference tracking · Relational representation learning · Discourse modeling · Spatially grounded motion generation

Status: Ongoing research direction; spatial modeling approaches and evaluation protocols are under investigation.

Discourse-Level Sign Language Translation

My translation research aims to move beyond isolated lexical recognition toward producing coherent written Chinese from continuous TSL utterances and discourse. Sign languages have their own linguistic structures; translating individual recognized signs into Chinese words is insufficient to preserve meaning, grammatical relations, and context.

I am investigating approaches that integrate visual motion representations with linguistic annotations and contextual information, including gloss sequences, semantic roles, spatially grounded referential loci, and discourse reference. A key direction is to explore natural language processing and large language models (LLMs) for context-aware translation, including both intermediate linguistic representations and more direct multimodal approaches. Important questions include how to preserve meaning across signed passages, handle ambiguous or incomplete visual evidence, and avoid generating unsupported content.

Sign language translation · Natural language processing · Large language models · Discourse context · Multimodal learning

Status: Research direction in development; an end-to-end discourse translation system has not yet been established.

Sign Language Generation and Avatar Motion Synthesis

My generation research explores how learned skeletal motion sequences can be used to drive articulated digital characters. I am developing experimental workflows that transfer captured or reconstructed signing movements to avatars, with an emphasis on preserving linguistically meaningful hand articulations, palm orientation, body motion, and coordinated two-handed signing.

Two central challenges are recovering motion that is partially hidden during capture and reconstructing three-dimensional movement from monocular two-dimensional observations. A single frontal video may not uniquely determine joint depth, hand ordering, or out-of-plane rotation. I am exploring temporal motion priors, kinematic constraints, and pose reconstruction to infer plausible three-dimensional configurations and retarget them to 2.5D or 3D characters without introducing anatomically implausible movements or losing important signing details. Beyond geometric fidelity, generation must also preserve linguistically meaningful spatial relationships and the consistent use of referential loci across a sequence.

In the longer term, I aim to investigate language-conditioned motion generation, in which written Chinese or structured linguistic representations guide the synthesis of meaningful signing sequences. This extends beyond replaying recorded motion and requires modeling the relationship between linguistic content, temporal structure, and visual expression.

Skeletal motion learning · Monocular 3D pose reconstruction · Motion retargeting · Avatar animation · Sign language generation

Status: Ongoing motion reconstruction and avatar prototype research; language-conditioned synthesis is a longer-term objective.

Research Methodology and Evaluation

My research draws on annotated TSL corpora, temporal landmark sequences, and collaboration with language specialists. The evaluation framework below outlines the questions and criteria I aim to investigate across the different stages of the research.

Motion Capture and Reconstruction

  • Landmark accuracy and temporal consistency
  • Recovery of occluded fingers and overlapping hands
  • Anatomical plausibility and preservation of linguistic handshape

Spatial Semantics and Reference Modeling

  • Preservation of linguistically relevant relative spatial relations across signers and recording conditions
  • Identification and tracking of annotated discourse referents and spatial loci where available
  • Consistency of spatial reference across utterances and generated signing sequences
  • Comparative evaluation of coordinate-only and spatially grounded representations

Recognition and Translation

  • Top-1 and Top-5 accuracy for isolated-sign recognition
  • Generalization across signers and recording conditions
  • Translation adequacy, contextual coherence, and preservation of meaning

Avatar Motion Generation

  • Motion fidelity and temporal continuity
  • Handshape, palm orientation, and two-hand spatial consistency
  • Intelligibility and linguistic fidelity assessed with signing expertise

Linguistic and Multimodal Data

  • Alignment of ELAN annotations with visual representations
  • Semantic roles, spatial relations, and discourse reference
  • Comparative evaluation of visual and linguistic feature representations
