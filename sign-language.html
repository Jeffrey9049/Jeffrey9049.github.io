Sign Language

Taiwan Sign Language research, multimodal machine learning, and accessible tools for language researchers and educators.

Motivation

My work aims to bridge the gap between sign language expertise and artificial intelligence. Sign language researchers and practitioners often have deep linguistic knowledge but may not have the programming background or technical resources needed to use machine learning systems. Conversely, AI developers may be unfamiliar with the linguistic and cultural knowledge essential to working responsibly with sign languages.

I seek to connect these fields by developing approachable tools and research workflows that help sign language specialists, educators, and members of signing communities work with technology. My goal is not only to improve computational methods for sign language research, but also to make those methods more accessible to people without programming experience.

Software Tools

Alongside my research, I develop practical software to lower the technical barriers to sign language data collection and analysis. These tools are intended to make common research tasks approachable for people with limited programming experience.

Landmark Studio

A visual interface for extracting motion landmarks from videos. Users can select the tracking components they need, such as hands, body pose, or face landmarks, and export structured data for further analysis without writing code.

Sign Language ML Studio

A beginner-friendly desktop environment for exploring machine learning with sign language examples. It is designed to help researchers and students prepare labeled data and try recognition workflows without having to build a machine learning pipeline from scratch.

Research Overview

Sign-language understanding requires information about handshape, palm orientation, location, movement, and temporal context. My research examines how these visual and linguistic dimensions can be represented in data-driven recognition models and how captured motion can be used for avatar-based reconstruction and visualization.

At the Graduate Institute of Linguistics, National Chung Cheng University, I contribute to multimodal corpus processing and machine-learning-based annotation and recognition workflows.

Current Work

Landmark-Based Sign Recognition

We extract body and hand landmarks from recorded signing and use temporal sequence models to recognize signed lexical items. Existing offline experiments use a bidirectional GRU and compare landmark-only representations with approaches incorporating aligned ELAN linguistic information.

MediaPipe · Spatiotemporal landmarks · Bidirectional GRU · PyTorch

Sign Language Avatar Modeling and Motion Reconstruction

I am developing an experimental workflow for reconstructing sign language movements from video and landmark data and transferring them to an articulated digital character. This work explores front-view avatar animation using body pose, hand landmarks, and motion-retargeting methods, including 2.5D and 3D representations.

A central challenge is preserving the linguistic details of signing: accurate handshapes, finger articulation, palm orientation, coordinated movements, and the relative depth of overlapping hands. The longer-term aim is to support visual demonstrations and accessible educational materials, while studying how faithfully digital avatars can represent recorded signing.

Human pose estimation · Hand tracking · Motion retargeting · 2.5D/3D avatars

Status: Ongoing research and prototype development.

Linguistically Informed Modeling

ELAN annotation tiers provide complementary descriptions of a sign, including gloss, handshape, palm orientation, location, and movement. I am interested in whether structured feature processing, attention mechanisms, and multi-task supervision improve recognition and generalization.

ELAN · Multimodal learning · Feature attention · Multi-task learning

Corpus Analysis

In addition to lexical recognition, I study computational workflows for analyzing annotated Taiwan Sign Language corpora, including semantic roles, discourse reference, and relationships between linguistic labels and movement observations.

Corpus processing · Linguistic annotation · Semantic analysis

Methods and Evaluation

Data and Modeling

  • Temporal landmark sequences from video
  • Gloss-aligned ELAN annotation tiers
  • Sequence recognition and representation learning
  • Feature-specific and multi-task approaches

Evaluation Questions

  • Top-1 and Top-5 lexical accuracy
  • Generalization across signers
  • Visually similar sign vocabulary
  • Robustness across recording conditions
